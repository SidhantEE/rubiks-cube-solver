Cube Solver Robot
Bill of Materials

Based on the proven t33devv/rubik build: six pancake NEMA 17 steppers in a 3D-printed frame, A4988 drivers on a breadboard, and an Arduino Mega. I added the parts its list leaves out: a 12 V supply, capacitors, a webcam for scanning, and filament.

One motor per face

Unfold the cube and every face has its own motor and its own pair of Mega pins. A move like R' spins only the right motor 90° counter-clockwise, which is 400 microsteps at 1/8 stepping. A planned firmware update will turn opposite faces such as U D at the same time.

3D-printed parts

Download the STLs from t33devv/rubik, in the 3D Files folder. That repo has no license, so print them for yourself but don't copy them into your repo. The repo doesn't say how many of each to print, so check the build video for counts.

Ways to change the cost

The t33devv list comes to about $173 assuming you already have tools. These swaps move the total up or down.

Already have a multimeter−$12
Skip the ring light and scan near a window−$12
Use a phone as the webcam (e.g. DroidCam)−$25
Enter colors by hand instead of scanning−$25
TMC2209 drivers instead of A4988 (much quieter)+$4
